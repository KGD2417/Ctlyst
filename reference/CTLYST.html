Talented founders stall — not for lack of merit, but at four practical walls.

i.

The Guidance Gap

Little or no access to people who have actually built and operated ventures — especially outside the major metros, where networks are thin.

ii.

The Capability Gap

Limited knowledge of the incubators, grants, subsidies, and government schemes that already exist — funded, waiting, and under-used.

The Trust Gap

Even where resources exist, founders cannot easily tell which are credible or how to navigate the process alone.

The Resource Ledger

The money is already there.
We help you reach it.

A reference to the major Government of India schemes we navigate with our founders — substantial, well-funded, and chronically under-used.

I.The Six Instruments
Scheme I

Startup India Seed Fund Scheme

Grants up to ₹20 lakh for prototyping and up to ₹50 lakh as seed funding, via approved incubators, from a ₹945 crore corpus. The prime early-stage resource.

Scheme II

Fund of Funds for Startups

A ₹10,000 crore corpus managed via SIDBI, funding SEBI-registered AIFs that invest in startups — indirect equity funding at the growth stage.

Scheme III

Credit Guarantee Scheme

Collateral-free loan guarantees for eligible startups, operational since April 2023 — debt without pledging your family's assets.

Scheme IV

Section 80-IAC Tax Benefit

A three-year income-tax deduction for eligible DPIIT-recognised startups — dramatically reducing the early tax burden after incorporation.

Scheme V

Atal Innovation Mission

Incubation centres, tinkering labs, and the "Mentors of Change" network under NITI Aayog — strong for hardware and social ventures at the grassroots.

Scheme VI

State Startup Policies

State-level grants, incubation, and dedicated funds — including Maharashtra's, directly relevant to our Mumbai launch city and beyond.

Indicative as of late 2025. Scheme terms, corpus sizes, and eligibility evolve — we verify current terms on the official Startup India portal before advising any founder.

Why This Page Exists

Awareness of these schemes remains uneven — lowest exactly where the need is highest: among first-time and small-city founders. That gap is where CTLYST stands.

II.A Worked Example

What navigation is worth.

Suppose a student team has a working concept for an agri-tech device but no money for a prototype. Alone, they might spend a year cold-emailing investors. With CTLYST, the path is different: we match them to a retired production engineer who refines the design for manufacturability, map them to the Seed Fund Scheme's prototype grant of up to ₹20 lakh through an approved incubator, and support the application end-to-end. If a founder secures a ₹5 lakh grant through us, our illustrative 5% success fee is ₹25,000 — paid from money they would never otherwise have found. The founder builds; the mentor contributes; the scheme fulfils its purpose. Everyone leaves the table better off.

The Roadmap

Evidence before expenditure.

We build nothing until real founders prove it worth building. This is the discipline we practise — and the discipline we teach.

I.The Road Ahead

Weeks 1–3 · Discovery

Fifteen to twenty honest conversations

Direct discovery talks with student and early founders. We listen for the single most common, most painful bottleneck — the one that recurs across many voices, not one-off complaints.

Weeks 3–6 · Micro-pilot

Two to three founders, helped end-to-end

Manual matches: a committed mentor plus three to five mapped schemes for each founder, with personal support on the next step. Success signal: at least one founder visibly moves forward.

Months 2–4 · First cohort

A small cohort and a first partner

A structured founder cohort, and one institutional or CSR partnership signed. Success signal: repeat demand, and the first paying relationship.

Months 4–12 · Platform

A lightweight platform, earned by evidence

An intake form, a mentor database, a searchable scheme library — automating only the steps proven necessary by hand. Success signal: a repeatable model that holds quality at lower effort.

Years 2–3 · Scale test

A second city, then a third

Replication of the city playbook, sustainable unit economics — or a clear-eyed pivot. We measure ourselves against outcomes, not optimism.

A Sobering Truth We Plan Around

Most ventures fail from running out of money or patience — not from a bad idea. We pace our spending and our promises accordingly.
